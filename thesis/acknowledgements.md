# Acknowledgements

I thank my supervisor, **dr inż. Michał Błędowski**, for agreeing to supervise this thesis and for the suggestion to extend the work with the comparative study reported in Chapter 4; the shape of the engineering and research halves of this project owes much to those conversations.

I am also grateful to the **Faculty of Electronics, Photonics and Microsystems** at Wrocław University of Science and Technology, and to the *Electronic and Computer Engineering* programme more broadly, for providing the structure that made it possible for an undergraduate project to attempt both a working system and an instrumented evaluation in the same volume.

This thesis was largely a solo effort, but it rests on the public work of people I never met — the maintainers of the open-source libraries the system runs on, and the authors of the papers cited in Chapters 1 and 4. Without their contributions, a single undergraduate could not have built and evaluated a system of this scope in the time available.

Writing the empirical chapters changed how I thought about the code, and rewriting the code changed how I framed the research questions. The discipline of carrying both halves in the same volume turned out to be the project's most useful constraint, and is, in retrospect, the thing I will most remember from this year.

To **Wrocław** — the city, the cohort I sat next to in lecture halls, and the small daily friction of studying far from home in a language not my own — thank you for shaping the conditions in which this work was possible.

Any errors that remain are my own.

Egemen Goncu
Wrocław, May 2026
