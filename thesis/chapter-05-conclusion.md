# Chapter 5 — Conclusions

This thesis presented the design, implementation, and empirical evaluation of a system for personalised career recommendation, scoped as a Bachelor-level engineering thesis at the Faculty of Electronics, Photonics and Microsystems of Wrocław University of Science and Technology.

## 5.1 Results

Three results were obtained.

**A deployed integrated system.** The system, deployed on Railway as a single application surface, integrates six analytical and generative tools — Resume Analyzer, Job Match, Career Path, Cover Letter, Interview Q&A, and Portfolio Planner — around a single shared representation of the candidate's resume. Chapter 2 documented the architecture, and Chapter 3 documented each of the six tools and the cross-cutting pipeline that they share. The system is operable from a laptop, demonstrable to an external reviewer without specialised tooling, and supports both authenticated user accounts and unauthenticated guest demonstrations.

**A defendable heuristic baseline.** Chapter 4 introduced the *strong heuristic v2*, a scoring pipeline whose individual components draw on published information-retrieval methods — TF–IDF–weighted keyword evidence and BM25 ranking [5, 6], ESCO-aligned skill normalisation [8, 9, 12], Levenshtein-based fuzzy matching [7] — combined with author-designed scoring formulas (section-weighted feature extraction with recruiter-side scanning informing the weight ordering, quantification regex with logarithmic saturation, and an action-verb dictionary aggregated from established career-services resources at Harvard FAS, MIT, and Princeton). The score-combination weights are author-selected and validated through the sensitivity analysis reported in Chapter 4.3.5 rather than through external survey citation. By construction, the heuristic is strictly non-neural, so the line between *heuristic* and *language-model* in the comparative study remains clean.

**A characterisation of the heuristic-versus-blended trade-off.** Section 4.3 reported four metric families measured across 100 (resume, job-description) pairs. On the overall score the heuristic mode tracks the blended mode at Pearson *r* = 0.727 and Spearman ρ = 0.708, both above the 0.7 reference threshold, while running roughly two orders of magnitude faster (median 36.5 ms versus 17.3 s) at zero LLM-side marginal cost. The agreement is dominated by the *keywords* axis (*r* = 0.941), where both modes consume the same evidence, and is weakest on the *clarity* axis (*r* = 0.475), where the language model contributes prose-quality evidence the heuristic does not see. Independently of the aggregate numbers, the blended mode produces qualitatively richer prose recommendations that the heuristic does not attempt to produce; this qualitative dimension is reserved for separate user-research investigation.

With the benefit of hindsight, the single decision the author would change is the order in which the tools were built. The first months of work went into the Resume Analyzer and the Job Match tool, each shipped as a self-contained pipeline with its own cache, its own persistence call, and its own observability boilerplate copy-pasted from the other. Only after the third tool joined the system did the duplication become painful enough to motivate the shared `run_tool_pipeline` refactor. A more disciplined approach would have written the pipeline first and built the first tool against it, even at the cost of a slightly slower start; a meaningful fraction of the early bugs in this project turned out to be variations on the same forgotten cache-key argument across three nearly-identical service modules.

## 5.2 Limitations

Three limitations of the work were stated in Chapter 4 and are repeated here for completeness.

The evaluation set is **single-language** (English) and **single-author** (one author synthesised all 30 resumes), so the empirical claim of Section 4.3 should be read as a within-author baseline rather than a cross-population conclusion. The blended mode uses a **single LLM provider** (Google Vertex AI Gemini 2.5 Flash), so the comparative result does not generalise across LLM choices without further measurement.

A fourth limitation is operational rather than methodological: the in-memory result cache (Chapter 3.1.5) is not shared between processes, which is acceptable at thesis-scope traffic but would not be at production scale. Migration to Redis is documented in Section 5.3 below.

A fifth, methodologically distinct limitation is that the system is currently a *thesis demonstration mode* rather than a sustainable commercial product. All six tools are fully accessible without an advertisement gate or a premium subscription. The hybrid monetisation strategy outlined in the project's specification document (`docs/spec.md`) is intentionally deferred to a post-thesis V1.1 release; this thesis does not address economic-viability questions beyond the per-call cost numbers reported in Section 4.3.4.

A sixth limitation is a deliberate scope reduction relative to the approved topic application. The application objective lists "skills, experience and *current job-market trends*" as the three input dimensions of the recommendation problem; the system implemented in this thesis incorporates the first two but does not model temporal job-market trend signals (for example, posting-frequency shifts of skills or roles, or hiring-rate dynamics by region). The career-recommendation, job-matching, and portfolio-planning tools therefore reason over the static representation of the candidate against an instantaneous representation of each job description, without a market-trend overlay. Extending the system with a trend-aware ranking layer is recorded as future work in Section 5.3.

## 5.3 Future development

Three directions for future work follow naturally from the limitations and from the architecture described in Chapter 2.

**Polish-language coverage.** The ESCO knowledge base used in the strong heuristic is published as a multilingual resource by the European Commission [17]. The current bundled subset uses only the English labels. Adding the Polish labels — and updating the resume parser, the keyword extractor, and the prompt builders to handle Polish text — would expand the system's relevance to its primary geographic context. The architectural change required is contained: a new pre-processing step in `app/services/quality_signals_v2.py`, a new prompt-locale branch, and an additional language-detection stage in the input sanitiser.

**Sentence-transformer intermediate tier.** Between the strict classical-IR heuristic and the language-model blended mode, a third option exists: a sentence-transformer–based semantic-matching tier, using a lightweight model such as `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` to compute resume–job cosine similarity directly. Recent research [20, 18, 21] has demonstrated that sentence-BERT–style embeddings outperform classical retrieval baselines on semantic-matching tasks while remaining one to two orders of magnitude faster than full-sized LLM inference and free of per-call third-party charges. The Stage H ablation reported in Section 4.3.6 of the present thesis probed exactly this question on the synthetic evaluation set: an SBERT semantic-fallback layer was implemented inside a `quality_signals_v3` ablation module alongside four other classical-IR enhancements (STAR-format detection, cross-axis coherence, n-gram phrase matching, and a 107 → 354 ESCO taxonomy expansion), and the per-variant Pearson correlation against the post-hoc-recovered LLM-only baseline was measured for each. The result is a controlled null with a small negative drift: every variant agrees less well with the LLM-only baseline than v2 does, with deltas spanning −0.001 to −0.038 — none of them statistically distinguishable from zero given the ±0.15 half-width of the cluster-bootstrap CI on the 100-pair set — and the SBERT-only variant in particular sits at *r* = 0.620 (Δ = −0.007 vs the *r* = 0.627 v2 baseline). The honest reading is that the residual cross-mode gap is not driven by deficiencies of the keyword-matching layer that an embedding model would close, but by prose-quality and role-fit reasoning that the language model accesses and that classical-IR enhancements of this class do not, on this evaluation set. Promoting the SBERT tier from an ablation cell to a deployed intermediate tier therefore remains future work, but the case for it is now contingent on a different evaluation surface — for example, real-world resume and job-description data with prose-quality variation that the synthetic-template construction suppresses — rather than on an open technical question about whether the technique works on the comparative-study benchmark reported here.

**Validity-threat closure that has already happened.** Three measurements that the original Stage F draft of this chapter listed as future work have since been completed in the Stage G+ APD-round revision and are now reported inline in Chapter 4. The *LLM-only baseline* per pair, recovered post-hoc from the locked-payload identity rather than from a second LLM rerun, is reported in Section 4.3.1' and addresses threat T2 of Section 4.1.4 (*r* (LLM-only, heuristic) = 0.627 [95% CI 0.476, 0.765]). The *vocabulary-disjoint regeneration of the synthetic benchmark* — under which the resume side and the job-description side draw from non-overlapping phrase pools by construction, with the per-pair content-token overlap audited at zero pairs above the >3-token threshold — is the dataset on which every number in Section 4.3 is computed and addresses T1. The *by-resume cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals* for all headline correlations, computed by resampling resume identifiers with replacement (`cluster_bootstrap_pearson` in `scripts/analyze_eval_results.py`, 2 000 resamples, seed = 42), are reported alongside the point estimates in Sections 4.3.1 and 4.3.1' and address T3. The three closures together support the *strong agreement* characterisation in Section 4.4 in place of the weaker *moderate-to-useful* phrasing the leakage-version draft supported.

**Retrieval-augmented variant of the blended mode.** The blended mode currently passes the resume and the job description directly into the language-model prompt. A retrieval-augmented variant [25] would, before each call, retrieve a small set of curated, source-attributed labour-market documents — recent salary surveys, role-progression standards, ESCO occupation descriptions — and inject them into the prompt as authoritative grounding. This would address one of the recurring criticisms of LLM-based career advice: that the model's output is opinionated but not source-attributed. The architecture supports this extension as an additional pre-LLM stage in the existing pipeline, without changes to the persistence model or the response contracts.

A fourth, smaller direction is the migration of the in-memory result cache to Redis, which is well-understood operationally and would unlock cross-process cache sharing once the deployment grows beyond a single backend instance. This is documented in `docs/spec.md` as a V1.1 item.

Among the three future-work directions, no single ranking holds across all the criteria that would matter. The sentence-transformer intermediate tier is the most technically *probed* of the three — Stage H of this thesis (Section 4.3.6) implemented an SBERT semantic-fallback layer alongside four other classical-IR enhancements and reported a controlled null on the synthetic evaluation set, with the SBERT-only variant at *r* = 0.620 (Δ = −0.007) and the full-stack v3 configuration at *r* = 0.588 (Δ = −0.038), all comfortably inside the ±0.15 half-width of the cluster-bootstrap noise band on the 100-pair set. The case for promoting the SBERT tier from an ablation cell to a deployed component therefore rests on a different evaluation surface than the comparative-study benchmark of Chapter 4 — likely a real-world resume and job-description corpus with prose-quality variation that the synthetic templates suppress. The retrieval-augmented blended mode is the most product-relevant of the three because the most consistent informal feedback on the deployed system has been a request for source attribution behind the recommendations. The Polish-language extension is the most regionally concrete one and would benefit from a native-Polish collaborator rather than from a single-author execution. Which of the three becomes the next deliverable depends on whether the project continues as a personal study, transitions into a paid product, or feeds into a future Master's-level extension.

The contributions of the thesis are an integrated, deployed system that brings six career tools together around a single resume representation, a defendable strictly-non-neural heuristic baseline, and an empirical characterisation of how that baseline compares to a language-model-augmented mode on score agreement, latency, and cost — within the synthetic evaluation set described in Chapter 4. The deployment is live at the time of submission and the administrative scoring-mode endpoint introduced in Chapter 4.2.9 is operable for live demonstration during the diploma defence.
