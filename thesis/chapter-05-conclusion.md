# Chapter 5 — Conclusions

> Target length: 3–5 pages (~1500 words). Mirrors the example thesis: results, limitations, future development.

---

This thesis presented the design, implementation, and empirical evaluation of a system for personalised career recommendation, scoped as a Bachelor-level engineering thesis at the Faculty of Electronics, Photonics and Microsystems of Wrocław University of Science and Technology.

## 5.1 Results

Three results were obtained.

**A production-grade integrated system.** The system, deployed on Railway as a single application surface, integrates six analytical and generative tools — Resume Analyzer, Job Match, Career Path, Cover Letter, Interview Q&A, and Portfolio Planner — around a single shared representation of the candidate's resume. Chapter 2 documented the architecture, and Chapter 3 documented each of the six tools and the cross-cutting pipeline that they share. The system is operable from a laptop, demonstrable to an external reviewer without specialised tooling, and supports both authenticated user accounts and unauthenticated guest demonstrations.

**A defendable heuristic baseline.** Chapter 4 introduced the *strong heuristic v2*, a scoring pipeline whose individual components draw on published information-retrieval methods — TF–IDF–weighted keyword evidence and BM25 ranking [27, 28], ESCO-aligned skill normalisation [17, 18, 21], Levenshtein-based fuzzy matching [29] — combined with author-designed scoring formulas (section-weighted feature extraction with recruiter-side scanning informing the weight ordering, quantification regex with logarithmic saturation, and an action-verb dictionary aggregated from established career-services resources at Harvard FAS, MIT, and Princeton). The score-combination weights are author-selected and validated through the sensitivity analysis reported in Chapter 4.3.5 rather than through external survey citation. By construction, the heuristic is strictly non-neural, so the line between *heuristic* and *language-model* in the comparative study remains clean.

**A characterisation of the heuristic-versus-blended trade-off.** Section 4.3 reported four metric families — score agreement (Pearson *r*, Spearman ρ, Kendall τ), score-distribution shape (mean, std, percentile range, KS distance), latency (median, 95th percentile, maximum), and per-call cost — measured by the evaluation harness across the (resume, job-description) pairs in the curated synthetic dataset. The exact numerical conclusion is given in Section 4.3 and is not duplicated here in order to keep this summary aligned with the measured values rather than with prior expectations. Independently of the specific numbers, the blended mode produces qualitatively richer prose recommendations that the heuristic does not attempt to produce; this qualitative dimension is reserved for separate user-research investigation.

With the benefit of hindsight, the single decision I would change is the order in which the tools were built. The first months of work went into the Resume Analyzer and the Job Match tool, each shipped as a self-contained pipeline with its own cache, its own persistence call, and its own observability boilerplate copy-pasted from the other. Only after the third tool joined the system did the duplication become painful enough to motivate the shared `run_tool_pipeline` refactor. If I started over, I would write that pipeline first and build the first tool against it, even at the cost of a slightly slower start; a meaningful fraction of the early bugs in this project turned out to be variations on the same forgotten cache-key argument across three nearly-identical service modules.

## 5.2 Limitations

Three limitations of the work were stated in Chapter 4 and are repeated here for completeness.

The evaluation set is **single-language** (English) and **single-author** (one author synthesised all 30 resumes), so the empirical claim of Section 4.3 should be read as a within-author baseline rather than a cross-population conclusion. The blended mode uses a **single LLM provider** (Google Vertex AI Gemini 2.5 Flash), so the comparative result does not generalise across LLM choices without further measurement.

A fourth limitation is operational rather than methodological: the in-memory result cache (Chapter 3.1.5) is not shared between processes, which is acceptable at thesis-scope traffic but would not be at production scale. Migration to Redis is documented in Section 5.3 below.

A fifth, methodologically distinct limitation is that the system is currently a *thesis demonstration mode* rather than a sustainable commercial product. All six tools are fully accessible without an advertisement gate or a premium subscription. The hybrid monetisation strategy outlined in the project's specification document (`docs/spec.md`) is intentionally deferred to a post-thesis V1.1 release; this thesis does not address economic-viability questions beyond the per-call cost numbers reported in Section 4.3.4.

## 5.3 Future development

Three directions for future work follow naturally from the limitations and from the architecture described in Chapter 2.

**Polish-language coverage.** The ESCO knowledge base used in the strong heuristic is published as a multilingual resource by the European Commission [17]. The current bundled subset uses only the English labels. Adding the Polish labels — and updating the resume parser, the keyword extractor, and the prompt builders to handle Polish text — would expand the system's relevance to its primary geographic context. The architectural change required is contained: a new pre-processing step in `app/services/quality_signals_v2.py`, a new prompt-locale branch, and an additional language-detection stage in the input sanitiser.

**Sentence-transformer intermediate tier.** Between the strict classical-IR heuristic and the language-model blended mode, a third option exists: a sentence-transformer–based semantic-matching tier, using a lightweight model such as `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` to compute resume–job cosine similarity directly. Recent research [23, 14, 25, 26] has demonstrated that sentence-BERT–style embeddings outperform classical retrieval baselines on semantic-matching tasks while remaining one to two orders of magnitude faster than full-sized LLM inference and free of per-call third-party charges. Integrating such a tier would produce a three-way comparison — classical heuristic, sentence-transformer, blended — and would test whether the qualitative gap between the heuristic and the blended modes can be partially closed without sacrificing the deterministic and zero-cost properties that make the heuristic baseline useful.

**Retrieval-augmented variant of the blended mode.** The blended mode currently passes the resume and the job description directly into the language-model prompt. A retrieval-augmented variant [33] would, before each call, retrieve a small set of curated, source-attributed labour-market documents — recent salary surveys, role-progression standards, ESCO occupation descriptions — and inject them into the prompt as authoritative grounding. This would address one of the recurring criticisms of LLM-based career advice: that the model's output is opinionated but not source-attributed. The architecture supports this extension as an additional pre-LLM stage in the existing pipeline, without changes to the persistence model or the response contracts.

A fourth, smaller direction is the migration of the in-memory result cache to Redis, which is well-understood operationally and would unlock cross-process cache sharing once the deployment grows beyond a single backend instance. This is documented in `docs/spec.md` as a V1.1 item.

Among the three future-work directions I do not yet have a single ranking that holds across all the criteria I would use. The sentence-transformer intermediate tier is the most technically interesting one — it would turn the binary comparative study reported in Chapter 4 into a three-way analysis and test whether the qualitative gap between the heuristic and the blended modes can be partially closed without paying an LLM cost on every request. The retrieval-augmented blended mode is the most product-relevant one because the most consistent informal feedback on the deployed system has been a request for source attribution behind the recommendations. The Polish-language extension is the most regionally concrete one but also the one I would most want to undertake with a native-Polish collaborator rather than alone. Which of the three becomes the next deliverable depends on whether the project continues as a personal study, transitions into a paid product, or feeds into a future Master's-level extension.

The contributions of the thesis are an integrated production-deployable system that brings six career tools together around a single resume representation, a defendable strictly-non-neural heuristic baseline, and an empirical characterisation of how that baseline compares to a language-model-augmented mode on score agreement, latency, and cost — within the synthetic evaluation set described in Chapter 4. The deployment is live at the time of submission and the administrative scoring-mode endpoint introduced in Chapter 4.2.9 is operable for live demonstration during the diploma defence.
